# Lecture Material

The following material is covered in the lectures:
* [L01](./L01/README.md): Combinational logic and first VHDL - gates, truth tables, Boolean
  algebra, CircuitVerse, and a complete `entity`/`architecture` module.
* [L02](./L02/README.md): Larger networks, multiplexers and submodules - a 4-to-1 mux introducing
  `process` and `case`, Karnaugh maps, building a design from more than one entity, and how to
  check your work with a testbench.
* [L03](./L03/README.md): Sequential logic - registers, D flip-flops, clocking, and edge
  detection.
* [L04](./L04/README.md): Metastability and synchronization - asynchronous inputs, synchronizers,
  debouncing, and generics.
* [L05](./L05/README.md): Variables and the hardware underneath - `signal` versus `variable`, and
  what synthesis actually builds on the FPGA.
* [L06](./L06/README.md): Counters and shift registers - wraparound, SIPO and PISO, and an 8-bit
  serial receiver.
* [L07](./L07/README.md): Timers - built by hand in CircuitVerse, then in VHDL, pacing a walking
  LED on the FPGA.
* [L08](./L08/README.md): State machines - designed by hand, then in VHDL, Moore and Mealy, with an
  FPGA demonstration.
